Grades

Homework assignments and exams will be combined to calculate a final grade in the following approximate proportions:
  • Midterm Exam: 20%
  • Final Exam: 30%
  • In-Class Work: 15%
  • Homework: 35%

Success

Homework

Part of this coursework includes regular short homework assignments, approximately one per week. These assignments are important as a way to engage with frequent practice with the material in preparation for the exam, as well as a method to provide regular feedback on your progress and understanding. As such, completing the assignments each week is CRUCIAL for your success in this course. ALWAYS do your best to turn in your best attempt on every assignment, as partial credit for imperfect answers may be given when appropriate.

Assignments will be turned in online, via Canvas. If you have multiple files to turn in, submit a single archive that includes all of them. If there is a written (or drawn) portion to the assignment, then turn in your written answers in a portable document file format (e.g. pdf, txt, markdown). Microsoft Word and other similar word processor formats should be converted to a portable format before turning in your answers. Alternatively, you may hand-write your answers on paper and submit a pdf or image file by scanning or taking a photo of your answers. If you submit a photo, ensure that all answers are clearly visible and legible. Illegible answers that cannot be read will be treated as no answer.

The Purpose of Homework

The purpose of the regular homework assignments is to give you an opportunity to work on examples in the topic area, with a range of difficulties from simple to complex, in your own time. Merely presenting a "correct" answer is NOT the main objective of the homework assignments. Instead, you are invited to think through the problems and develop your understanding, like physical exercise and training, so that you will be prepared for the real test: exams and, eventually, the rest of the world.

Because it is easier than ever to "find" correct answers with tools like generative AI, just showing the answer is not enough. Instead, for every homework exercise, you must explain your reasoning, in your own words, on how you got that answer and why it is correct. Moreover, you should be prepared to explain this reasoning, orally and in-person, on how you approached any given answer, if necessary. If you use tools to help you arrive at the correct answer, you still need to explain the how and why, so that you are ready for more challenging, real-world versions of the simple exercises. Therefore, the homework grade will be based primarily on the reasoning explaining the answer, and not the technical correctness of the answer itsef. The intention of this grading policy is give you the freedom to explore the problem fully, building intuition and understanding, without the pressure of arriving at the only correct answer as fast as possible.

Deadlines

All assignments must be completed and turned in on-time by their due date. Unless otherwise specified, the time of all due dates is before midnight of that day.

For the sake of fairness among all students, and to accommodate unexpected emergencies that might keep you from finishing your homework on time, you will start with an initial pool of 10 FREE LATE DAYS to extend a deadline by UP TO 4 DAYS at a time. Every time you want turn in an assignment n < 4 days late, you can use n days from your pool of free missed deadline days, and it will be graded without penalty no-questions-asked. If you turn in an assignment n > 4 days late, then you can use the maximum 4 days from your pool at a time, and start accruing the 10% per day pentalty (as described below) for the remaining n - 4 days after the deadline.

Otherwise, if you do not have enough days to make up for a missed deadline, or miss a deadline by more than 4 days, your late assignment will accrue a 10% (additive) penalty for every calendar day after the due date. For example, an assignment scoring 95% that is 3 days late (with no free late days remaining) accrues a 30% penalty, and will be counted as an adjusted 65%. After 10 days, this penalty adds up to 100% and credit for the assignment is capped at 0 points.

In very exceptional cases, additional extensions to due dates may be granted at the discretion of the instructor if a student has unusual circumstances (such as long-term health issues) that would reasonably interfere with the on-time completion of several assignments throughout the course of the entire semester. To ensure that all students can be fairly accommodated, these exceptional extensions must be requested well in advance of the due date in question, as soon as possible. Long-term exceptional accomodations are not emergencies, and will not be granted after a due date is already passed.

Ordinary illness, short-term personal emergencies, technical difficulties, or other school or work obligations (especially on the day of the due date) do not count as exceptional circumstances, and are covered by your free late days. If you miss a due date for this kind of reason and are out of free late days, requests for additonal non-exceptional extensions to avoid late penalties will be denied. So be sure to budget your free missed deadline days wisely to help you in these situations.

Attendance

Students are expected to attend class regularly, as regular attendance is one of the most important contributors to student success. However, students may occasionally need to miss class due to illness, emergency, or caring for a sick family member. In such cases, you are responsible for notifying me of your absences and working with me to arrange to make up any missed work.

Occasionally, there will be in-class exercises that will be given out and completed entirely within the normal class meeting times. Participation with in-class exercises (such as turning in solutions or work) will count toward the final grade. These activities will not be announced in advance, and missed in-class exercises cannot be made up afterward in the case of an absence. Therefore, it is every student's responsibility to keep regular attendance to be able to participate. Because a small amount of unplanned absences are expected to happen (such as due to sickness), the lowest two in-class exercises will be dropped from the final grade.

As with deadlines described above, if you anticipate the need for long-term accomodations for an alternative to in-class exercises throughout the term (more than 2 absences), then you must arrange for accomodations with the instructor in advance.

A note on Zoom Participation: Classroom lectures will be recorded, and available for (re)viewing afterward. Occasionally, certain lectures may be moved online instead of the regular in-person meeting. These special online lectures will be announced by email in advance. Because I believe that the class will be more engaging if we can see one another, I strongly recommend that students keep their cameras on during a virtual class unless you have a valid reason not to, which you should discuss with me ahead of time.

    Rules

    Academic Integrity and Plagiarism

    All students are advised that there is a University policy regarding academic integrity. Students are responsible for the honest completion and representation of their work. It is the students’ responsibility to familiarize themselves with these policies, as any misconduct will be handled according to the University policy.

    Students are welcome discuss the exercises with one another. However, each student is responsible for their own work and completing all exercises independently. Copying another students solutions (either by sending files directly or copying down from another's' screen or paper), or from some other source (e.g. the internet), and turning them in as their own work constitutes plagiarism, and will result in a failing grade. What specific actions constitute plagiarism is left to the discretion of the instructor, and will not be fully enumerated here. If in doubt, please ask before doing something that seems suspicious.

    Posting answers to homework exercises to any publicly-visible website or location also counts as plagiarism. Future students deserve the chance to fully engage with the homework assignments on their own. If you are found publicly posting any answers to a homework assignment, it will be treated the same as if you provided your answers to another student of this same class.

    AI Usage

    Homework assignments in this class are designed as simplified problems that are small enough for you to work on in a short span of time, rather than real, practical programming projects. Therefore, for the purposes of academic integrity and plagiarism, all generative AI tools (such as large langauge modles like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini) will be considered the same as other students, in this class or otherwise. For such small practice problems, having them solved automatically defeats the purpose.

    For example, sending a copy of a homework assignment to to Claude, asking it to solve the problems, and then copying the answers as your own is considered plagiarism, and will be handled as such. Alternatively, using Claude as a tutor aide by asking questions about the problems and looking for early feedback and corrections to arrive at the answer for yourself is an okay use of AI tools. Both of these use-cases reflect the same policy as when consulting other students in the class: copying their answers is plagiarism and asking for help to do your own work is valid and encouraged.

    Since every answer to a homework exercise must be accompanied by some rational explaining how you arrived at that answer, this lets you provide your proof of work that you were actively engaged in solving the homework problems rather than copying from somewhere else. In case there is a question of plagiarism, you may be asked to explain that rational, orally and in-person, to demonstrate how you worked on the homework.

    Credit Hour Policy

    Federal definition of a credit hour requires that for every course credit awarded, a course must offer 15 hours of instructor-led course activities and 30 hours of out-of-class student work. This means that a standard 3 credit hour course requires 45 hours of instructor-led course activities and 90 hours of out-of-class student work.

    University Privacy Statement

    UMass Lowell recognizes the importance of mutual trust between students and faculty. Massachusetts is a two-party consent state, which means it is illegal to record someone without their permission. Recordings of classroom lectures are the intellectual property of the instructor. Instructors have the right to prohibit audio and video recording of their lectures, unless the requesting student is registered with Disabilities Services and recording of class sessions is an approved accommodation. In addition, sharing or selling recordings of classroom activities, discussions or lectures with any other person or medium without permission of the instructor is prohibited.
